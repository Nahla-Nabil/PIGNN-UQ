\title{Physics-Informed Graph Neural Networks with Uncertainty
Quantification: A Pre-Registered Evaluation Protocol for Blue Carbon
Credit Pricing}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Nahla Nabil Skaik}
\IEEEauthorblockA{Arab Open University, Bahrain\\
nahla.nabil.52@gmail.com}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Flat, rule-based non-permanence buffers price every credit in a voluntary
carbon market the same way regardless of how confident the underlying
carbon-stock estimate is, so a well- and a poorly-characterized site are
discounted identically. We test whether a physics-informed graph neural
network (GNN) combined with conformalized quantile regression (CQR) can
produce a per-site confidence signal to complement it, on the Ten Thousand Islands
mangrove fringe, southwest Florida (a 10,124-node spatial graph
from Sentinel-2, Global Mangrove Watch, and field-calibrated soil organic
carbon). Model selection (physics-loss weight, feature set, calibration
method) used a pre-registered protocol on validation only, confirmed once
on a held-out test block never used for any prior decision. A five-seed
robustness check found the physics-informed loss's point-accuracy
advantage over a no-physics GNN, reported in an earlier single-seed
pilot, did not replicate: on test, the no-physics GNN has the best
$R^2$ of three models ($-0.383$ vs.\ $-0.430$ physics-informed and
$-0.450$ linear). The physics-informed model's predictions correlate most
strongly with the imposed physical prior on held-out nodes ($0.755$ vs.\
$0.682$/$0.686$), evidence the constraint transfers under spatial shift,
not independent physical validation; this gap is real but substantially
smaller than the earlier single-seed estimate. Two CQR calibration
methods were compared: global achieves 91.1\%/96.4\% coverage at
90\%/95\% nominal; a spatially localized (Mondrian) variant,
pre-registered from validation-only evidence, undercovers more on test
(83.4\%, 92.4\%). This is a genuine negative result, consistent with
spatial-conformal theory requiring more local calibration density than
this set supplied. A pricing PoC, built on the better-calibrated global
intervals, maps interval width to a multiplier ranging
$0.50$--$1.50$ across five test nodes, against one fixed $0.800$
multiplier under Plan Vivo's flat 20\% buffer. Point-prediction accuracy
remains weak throughout; the contribution is a
pre-registered protocol that catches both a single-run estimate and a
calibration refinement overstating their own reliability, not validated
point accuracy or a validated pricing model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Voluntary carbon markets discount mangrove restoration and conservation
credits for non-permanence risk (carbon later released via erosion,
sea-level rise, or disturbance) using a buffer pool: a fixed percentage
of issued credits withheld from sale. Verra's VM0033 scores non-permanence
risk via a checklist-based tool, and Plan Vivo V5.0 applies a flat 20\%
buffer, revisited roughly every ten years
\cite{verra_vm0033,planvivo_buffer}. Neither resolves risk at
sub-project granularity or conditions the discount on stock-estimate
confidence. A restored interior stand and an eroding fringe in the same
project get the identical discount, a market inefficiency as much as an
academic one. Carbon-stock models for this ecosystem and region also
report point estimates with no calibrated uncertainty: Radabaugh et al.'s
field-plot-calibrated spatial model for our pilot region
\cite{radabaugh2023bluecarbon}, Sanderman et al.'s global mangrove SOC
product, our training label \cite{sanderman2023soc}, and Simard et al.'s
global mangrove biomass product \cite{simard2019mangrove} report one
value per pixel/site, with no per-prediction confidence for pricing to
condition on. Conformal prediction gives valid, distribution-free
uncertainty for earth-observation regression \cite{eo_conformal2024} and
for spatial prediction under autocorrelation-induced exchangeability
violations \cite{lou2024geoconformal}; deep ensembles have been shown in
an adjacent climate-downscaling setting \cite{gonzalezabad2023ensembles}.
However, neither literature pairs calibrated uncertainty with a
physics-informed spatial predictor, couples it to a buffer-pool pricing
mechanism, or asks whether a calibration strategy (global versus
spatially localized) stays valid under a single region's calibration-
density constraints. That is the narrower gap this paper addresses; the
broader multi-region MRV agenda is a separate, larger question the wider
literature is only beginning to take up
\cite{roy2024mangroveasiapacific}. This paper combines a
physics-informed graph neural network with distribution-free uncertainty
calibration to test whether estimation confidence can complement, not
replace, a flat non-permanence discount, following physics-informed
neural networks \cite{raissi2019pinn} and
their graph-based extensions \cite{sanchezgonzalez2020gns}, applied to
mass-conserving flood forecasting \cite{taghizadeh2025floodpignn,
dualfloodgnn2025} but not, to our knowledge, blue carbon.

The scope is deliberately narrow: one pilot region, one physics constraint
(a steady-state mass-balance proxy, not a full temporal flux equation),
and one primary UQ method (CQR; deep ensembles and MC dropout are left
for future work). Every model-selection decision, physics-loss weight,
feature set, calibration method, was pre-registered on a validation
split and confirmed once on a spatially distinct test block never used
for any prior decision; we treat this two-stage protocol itself as a
methodological contribution (Section~III-C), since a naive single-run
estimate and a theoretically-motivated calibration refinement each turned
out to mislead here (Section~V). What survives that scrutiny is smaller
and more tightly bounded than either initial result suggested: a real
but modest prior-generalization advantage for the physics-informed model,
and evidence that localized (Mondrian) conformal calibration, plausible
in theory and competitive on validation-internal evidence, underperforms
the simpler global variant on this AOI's test data. The contribution is
this pre-registered evaluation and its honest limits, not a validated
point-prediction accuracy claim or a validated pricing model.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Physics-informed GNNs.} Physics-guided ML integrates
scientific structure into learned models \cite{willard2022piml}; PINNs add
a PDE-residual loss \cite{raissi2019pinn}, Graph Network Simulators encode
physics via local, permutation-invariant message passing
\cite{sanchezgonzalez2020gns}. The closest prior art, flood forecasting
\cite{taghizadeh2025floodpignn, dualfloodgnn2025}, is entirely
hydrological and does not target mangrove carbon or couple prediction to
a priced instrument via UQ.

\textbf{Mangrove carbon stock estimation.} The dominant approach is
per-pixel tabular regression (random forest, XGBoost) on spectral
indices. Simard et al.'s global mangrove biomass product
\cite{simard2019mangrove} was inaccessible here (NASA Earthdata
login required). Radabaugh et al.\ built a field-plot-calibrated, 30\,m
carbon-stock model for the southwest Florida coast, one sub-region
matching our pilot AOI \cite{radabaugh2023bluecarbon}. Sanderman et
al.\ produced the global, field-calibrated 30\,m SOC product
\cite{sanderman2023soc} we train against. A 105-study review confirms
belowground-carbon and valuation research remain under-studied versus
aboveground biomass \cite{roy2024mangroveasiapacific}. None uses a graph
representation or a physical constraint, or reports a calibrated
uncertainty interval (the gap this paper addresses).

\textbf{Uncertainty quantification.} MC dropout \cite{gal2016dropout} is
cheap but poorly calibrated under shift; deep ensembles
\cite{lakshminarayanan2017ensembles} calibrate better but need multiple
full models, as in climate-downscaling \cite{gonzalezabad2023ensembles}.
CQR \cite{romano2019cqr} gives a finite-sample, distribution-free coverage
guarantee under exchangeability, validated for earth observation
\cite{eo_conformal2024}; that guarantee is conditional on exchangeability
spatial autocorrelation can violate. GeoConformal Prediction
\cite{lou2024geoconformal} addresses this with a spatially localized
framework, motivating the Mondrian variant we test (Section~III-F).

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Pilot region and data}
The pilot AOI is the Ten Thousand Islands / Rookery Bay mangrove fringe,
southwest Florida ($-81.90^\circ$ to $-81.40^\circ$ lon.,
$25.90^\circ$ to $26.30^\circ$ lat.), chosen for free access to every
needed layer: Sentinel-2 L2A via Microsoft Planetary Computer,
Global Mangrove Watch (GMW) v3 extent
\cite{bunting2022gmw}, the Sanderman et al.\ SOC product
\cite{sanderman2023soc}, and a NOAA CO-OPS tide gauge (station 8725114).
Field-calibrated validation numbers came from Radabaugh
et al.\ \cite{radabaugh2023bluecarbon}.

\subsection{Graph construction}
Native 30\,m SOC grid cells were aggregated into $5\times5$ blocks
($\sim$150\,m nodes); Sentinel-2 bands were resampled onto this grid
(bilinear) and the GMW mask (nearest-neighbor), so the label was
never resampled. A node was kept if its block-mean GMW mangrove fraction
exceeded 0.5 and it had a valid SOC value, giving 10{,}124 nodes and
64{,}366 directed edges (8-connectivity). Node features were NDVI,
raw B04/B08/B11 reflectance, and distance to the tide gauge; the label
was mean SOC (t\,C/ha, 0--100\,cm). An initial contiguous-band split
gave negative test $R^2$ for every model family, since the
held-out band straddled a real regional carbon-density gradient
(Radabaugh et al.'s sub-regional figures range $265.1\pm43.2$ to
$409\pm104.4$\,Mg\,C\,ha$^{-1}$ \cite{radabaugh2023bluecarbon}). We
replaced this with an $8\times8$ checkerboard grid of spatial superblocks
($\sim$6\,km each, far larger than the 150\,m node spacing), assigned by
cumulative node count rather than superblock count, since mangrove nodes
concentrate in a thin coastal fringe.

\subsection{Test-set protocol and pre-registered selection}
An early version of this evaluation used one fixed split, directly
comparing test-set metrics across model variants to decide the paper's
central claim, using test data beyond its role as a single untouched
confirmation. We corrected this before any
experiment below: a fresh validation/test block was carved from
superblocks never individually inspected for a decision (only touched via
gradient updates); the previously-inspected split was folded into the new
training pool, since training on previously-seen data does not leak, but
evaluating on it again would. The corrected split is 6{,}465 train /
1{,}583 validation / 2{,}076 test nodes (label means 427.9/421.2/399.4\,t/ha,
AOI mean 421.0). Every later selection decision (physics-loss
weight, feature set, calibration method) was made on validation only,
by rules fixed beforehand, confirmed once on test
(Section~IV): a pre-registered "tested $X$, selected $Y$" claim this
protocol makes defensible, surfacing a genuine instability a fixed split
could not reveal.

\subsection{Architecture and physics-informed loss}
All models shared a 2-layer GraphSAGE backbone \cite{hamilton2017graphsage} (hidden dim.\ 16, dropout
0.3), Adam (lr $=0.01$, weight decay $10^{-3}$), early stopping on
validation MSE (patience 30). Baseline and physics-informed variants
differed only in loss function, isolating the physics term's effect. The
full mass-balance relationship is
$dC/dt = \text{Accretion} - \text{Export} - \text{Decomposition}$; a
single Sentinel-2 date and no flux-tower data ruled out estimating this
directly. We imposed a steady-state prior instead: predicted stock was
softly encouraged toward an equal-weighted, min-max-normalized (training
data only) combination of an accretion proxy (NDVI) and an export proxy
(tide-gauge distance, a positional stand-in for tidal energy absent a
per-node hydrodynamic layer). Both proxy components are also raw model
features (Section~III-B); withholding both from a no-physics baseline
reduces its test prior-generalization correlation only modestly ($0.682$
to $0.654$), so this is not narrowly a feature-overlap artifact. A second
term penalized negative
predictions, a mass-balance corollary, not independent.
Both combined into one physics loss, added to the standard MSE data loss,
with no fitted coefficients inside the proxy itself:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{phys}} = \lambda \cdot \text{MSE}(\hat y, \text{proxy})
+ w_{\text{nonneg}} \cdot \text{mean}\!\left(\text{ReLU}(-\hat y_{\text{raw}})^2\right)
\label{eq:physloss}
\end{equation}
$w_{\text{nonneg}}=0.1$ was held fixed
(never swept); $\lambda$ was chosen by a pre-registered sweep over
$\{0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$, five seeds each, on validation
only, selecting the value with best mean validation prior-generalization
correlation within 0.05 of the best mean validation $R^2$.
This rule selected $\lambda=0.1$ (mean val.\ $R^2=-0.007$, mean val.\
prior-gen.\ corr.\ $=0.670$), not the ad hoc $\lambda=0.3$ from an
earlier pilot pass, which the rule did not select (full 7-value table
in the project's number-source log).

\subsection{Uncertainty quantification: localized CQR}
A quantile-head GraphSAGE (same backbone) was trained with pinball loss
on the 2.5th, 5th, 95th, and 97.5th percentiles, data loss only (no
physics loss; combining both was future work). Two feature sets,
NDWI (McFeeters, Green/NIR) and NDMI (Gao, NIR/SWIR), were tested as
additions to base, five seeds each, on validation only, under the same
tolerance rule as $\lambda$; neither improved mean validation prior-gen.\
correlation within tolerance, so base was retained (Section~IV-A).

Split-conformal calibration used the validation set: the conformity score
$E_i=\max(q_{\text{lo}}(x_i)-y_i,\, y_i-q_{\text{hi}}(x_i))$ was computed
there, and the $\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil/n$ quantile of
$\{E_i\}$ adjusted the interval bounds (\emph{global} calibration, one
correction for every node). A \emph{localized (Mondrian)} variant instead
partitioned validation nodes by spatial superblock (Section~III-B),
computing a separate correction per block ($\geq$30 points, else global
fallback). Both, plus a density-weighted variant (Gaussian kernel,
bandwidth = median nearest-neighbor distance), were compared on a
validation-internal holdout (val split 50/50 into calibration/check,
never reading test; Section~IV-B). Localized (Mondrian) was carried
forward to test; global was the comparator, density-weighted a
non-adopted check.

\section{Experiments and Results}

This section reports measurements only; their interpretation is deferred
to Section~V.

\subsection{Multi-seed robustness and selection (validation only)}
The three-way comparison at the earlier pilot's fixed weight ($\lambda=0.3$,
$w_{\text{nonneg}}=0.1$) was re-run across five seeds $\{1,\dots,5\}$ on
validation data. Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a) plots mean $\pm$ one std.\ dev.\
for validation $R^2$ and prior-gen.\ correlation. Baseline GNN: $R^2$
mean $=0.005$, std $=0.019$; corr.\ mean $=0.544$, std $=0.056$.
Physics-informed GNN: $R^2$ mean $=-0.040$, std $=0.026$; corr.\ mean
$=0.632$, std $=0.137$. Linear regression is deterministic: $R^2=-0.030$,
corr.\ $=0.486$ (no error bar; no seed dependence to show). The
$\lambda$ sweep and NDWI/NDMI ablation (Section~III-E/F) selected
$\lambda=0.1$ and the base feature set mechanically, by the rules stated
where each is defined.

\subsection{Localized vs.\ global CQR (validation-internal, pre-test)}
On the validation-internal calibration/check split (Section~III-F), at
90 and 95\% nominal respectively: global calibration reached 88.0 and
95.6\% empirical coverage (mean width 307.6 and 367.8\,t/ha); localized
(Mondrian) reached 92.2 and 95.6\% (width 305.3 and 358.2\,t/ha);
density-weighted reached 81.1 and 84.7\% (width 220.2 and 241.5\,t/ha),
narrower but visibly undercovering both levels.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{The physics term's point-accuracy advantage did not replicate.}
An earlier single-seed pilot found the physics-informed loss improved test
$R^2$ over a no-physics baseline. The multi-seed check (Section~IV-A)
already showed this was fragile (physics-informed mean validation $R^2$
across 5 seeds ($-0.040$) sat below the baseline's ($+0.005$) even before
the corrected split and pre-registered $\lambda$), and the final test
evaluation confirms it: the no-physics baseline GNN has the best test
$R^2$ of the three. This is the actual finding, not reconciled with the
earlier result by reframing; a single seed on a single split was not
reliable here.

\textbf{What does hold up, at a smaller margin.} The physics-informed
model's predictions correlate with the imposed mass-balance proxy more
strongly than either non-physics model on every evaluation in this paper,
multi-seed validation (0.632 vs.\ 0.544/0.486) and the single test
evaluation (0.755 vs.\ 0.682/0.686). This requires the same precise
interpretation as before: the proxy correlated against is the exact
quantity the physics loss trained toward, so this is a
\emph{prior-generalization} check (whether the constraint transfers to
held-out nodes), not independent physical validation, which this AOI's
single-date, flux-tower-free data cannot supply. Whether this correlation
reflects genuine physical behavior, not just successful optimization
against its own training target, is this paper's most significant open
question. Resolving it would require flux-tower or repeated field-plot
carbon-flux measurements at this AOI, which do not currently exist, the
clearest concrete next step for extending this work beyond a proxy-based
prior. What changed under the corrected protocol is the effect's size,
not its direction: a real but modest margin on test (0.069--0.073), not
the 0.6-point gap the
single-seed pilot reported. This is a direct product of the pre-registered
protocol working as intended.

\textbf{Localized calibration did not improve on global, and that is
itself informative.} Section~III-F pre-registered localized (Mondrian)
CQR from validation-internal evidence, before test was read. On test it
undercovered more than global at both levels (83.4\% vs.\ 90\% nominal;
92.4\% vs.\ 95\%, against global's 91.1\%/96.4\%), likely because only 6
spatial superblocks had enough validation nodes ($\geq 30$) for a stable
local correction. This matches theory: spatial conformal validity requires
an \emph{infill asymptotic} regime with sufficient calibration-point
density per local region \cite{mao2024validmodelfree}, plausibly what
our split validation set lacked, a density limit, not evidence against
localization. Nor is the pattern isolated to this paper:
applying Mondrian calibration to real-estate valuation, Hjort et al.\
\cite{hjort2023spatialavm} report "little to no improvement" over global
in one scenario, cautioning that an apparent gain can instead reflect a
non-conformity score failing to capture spatial trends. Localization
gains in asset-pricing conformal calibration are setting-dependent, not
automatic. \textbf{Business implication:} global (simpler, the only
method meeting nominal coverage on test) is defensible for near-term
deployment; Section~VI's pricing PoC uses global intervals accordingly.
Both methods still produce test-set widths (318--383\,t/ha) several times the
label's std.\ dev.\ (87.6\,t/ha), wide, not falsely confident.

\section{Pricing Proof-of-Concept}

CQR interval width measures present-day estimation confidence, not
non-permanence risk (future carbon loss to erosion, disturbance,
sea-level rise) -- the quantity buffer pools price; this paper does not
establish the two correlate. We illustrate,
without validating, how per-node estimation confidence could sit
alongside, not replace, a non-permanence buffer, built on \emph{global}
CQR intervals per Section~V's recommendation, not the localized (Mondrian)
variant it cautions against. A relative interval width is the 90\%-CQR
(global) width divided by the test set's own median (341.1\,t/ha; range
143.9--552.5\,t/ha). A price multiplier is
$1 - \alpha(\text{relative width}-1)$, clipped to $[0.5, 1.5]$, with
$\alpha=1.0$ an arbitrary illustrative sensitivity, not fit to market
outcome. Table~\ref{tab:pricing} shows five nodes against Plan Vivo
V5.0's flat 20\% buffer.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Single-date imagery} constrains the physics term to a
cross-sectional relationship, not an actual $dC/dt$ (not invalidating
the prior-generalization result, defined relative to that same proxy).
\textbf{No flux/decomposition data} exists for this AOI; accretion/export
are proxies (Section~III-E). \textbf{Distance-to-gauge is a positional
proxy}, not a tidal-energy layer. \textbf{The prior-generalization result
is not independent validation} (Sections~III-F, V): it measures transfer,
not physical correctness. \textbf{CQR excluded the physics loss}, a
disclosed scope choice. \textbf{Localized (Mondrian) CQR undercovered on
test} (Section~IV-C, V), a data-density limit here, not spatial
calibration generally \cite{lou2024geoconformal,mao2024validmodelfree}.
\textbf{Single region}; the five-seed check addresses seed variance, not
regional generalization. \textbf{The pricing mechanism is illustrative},
with an unfit sensitivity parameter and no market validation.
\textbf{The graph has 2{,}520 directed edges (3.92\%) crossing split
boundaries} (9.7\% of nodes, 14.2\% of test nodes, are 1-hop neighbors
across a split); restricting Table~\ref{tab:threeway} to the 1{,}782
non-boundary test nodes gives lower $R^2$ for all three models (linear
$-0.504$, baseline GNN $-0.447$, physics GNN $-0.494$) but the same
ranking, so reported numbers carry a small, non-model-specific optimistic
bias from this leakage. \textbf{The single test-side seed, the untested random-forest baseline,
and the pricing mechanism's gaming-risk and registry-adoption questions}
remain unaddressed.

\section{Conclusion}

A pre-registered, two-stage protocol (validation-only selection, single
held-out test confirmation) caught two overstated results a single run
would have missed: an earlier single-seed pilot's headline finding, and a
theoretically-motivated localized calibration refinement favorable on
validation-internal evidence alone. What survives (the physics-informed
model's smaller but real prior-generalization advantage, and honestly
wide, node-differentiated CQR intervals) are two separate results, not
one flowing from the other: the CQR pipeline priced here used data loss
only, without the physics term. Together they motivate the market-facing
contribution: an estimation-confidence signal that could complement, not
replace, flat buffer pools, deployed here on the simpler,
better-calibrated global variant. Future work: a second
Sentinel-2 date, a higher-volume or second-region localized-conformal
test, deep-ensemble/MC-dropout comparisons, and market-outcome validation
of the pricing mechanism against non-permanence risk.
