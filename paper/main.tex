\title{PIGNN-UQ: Physics-Informed Graph Neural Networks with Uncertainty Quantification for Registry-Compatible Blue Carbon Crediting}

\begin{abstract}
Mangrove carbon credits are discounted by a non-permanence buffer that does not depend on how uncertain the underlying carbon-stock estimate is.
In this study, PIGNN-UQ combines a physics-informed graph neural network with gradient-boosted trees to map soil organic carbon over two southwest Florida mangrove regions (10,124 and 22,326 nodes of 150\,m), and conformal prediction turns each estimate into a calibrated 90\% interval that feeds the uncertainty deduction of the Verra VM0033 methodology.
Spatial cross-validation selected the mean of the physics-informed network and XGBoost, which reached a test $R^2$ of 0.28 and 0.60, against 0.20 and 0.59 for XGBoost alone.
The intervals covered 91.5\% and 89.1\% of test nodes, and their width ranked absolute errors with a Spearman correlation of 0.54 in the first region.
The registry rule withheld a median 12.7\% and 2.1\% of credits per node, close to the 11.0\% median buffer held by the seven blue carbon projects that have issued credits, whereas the label map's own uncalibrated intervals would withhold about 65\%.
Aggregating node uncertainty under the methodology's independent-error assumption covered only 18\% and 33\% of realized project-level errors, while a correlation-aware aggregation covered at least 91\%.
At 25 field sites with 87 soil cores, the median error of the ensemble was 10.1\%, below a regional mean (15.0\%) and two published carbon maps (14.1\% and 15.1\%).
These results support crediting rules that keep estimation uncertainty separate from non-permanence risk and aggregate it under spatially correlated errors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Under Verra's VM0033 methodology, a mangrove project deposits part of its credits in a pooled buffer account against non-permanence risk (carbon later released through erosion, sea-level rise or disturbance), and a separate deduction applies when the 90\% confidence interval of the estimate exceeds 20\% of its value \cite[p.~64]{verra_vm0033}.
The buffer share comes from a risk assessment and does not depend on how well the carbon stock itself was estimated.
Published mangrove carbon maps either report no per-site interval or report intervals whose coverage and crediting effect have not been checked \cite{maxwell2023soc,radabaugh2023bluecarbon}, and the methodology combines plot uncertainties under an independence assumption that spatial models rarely satisfy.

This paper asks whether calibrated, per-site uncertainty from a spatial model can supply that deduction in a way a registry could audit.
The proposed pipeline, PIGNN-UQ (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}), combines a physics-informed graph neural network (GNN) that encodes neighborhood structure and a tidal-inundation prior, tree ensembles as strong tabular baselines, and conformalized quantile regression for distribution-free intervals.
This study departs from earlier work in four ways: (1) the interval of every node is converted into the VM0033 deduction, kept separate from the non-permanence buffer and compared with the buffers held by issuing projects; (2) the methodology's own aggregation rule is tested empirically and shown to understate project-level uncertainty when model errors are spatially correlated; (3) predictions and published maps are checked against 87 independent field soil cores; and (4) the analysis covers two regions, two image dates and leakage-free spatial splits, with every model choice fixed on validation data under rules committed before the experiments were run.

The scope is deliberately applied: the paper does not propose a new network architecture.
The contribution is a crediting pipeline whose uncertainty is calibrated, auditable and conservative, together with evidence on where that uncertainty is and is not informative.
Table~\ref{tab:gap} positions this contribution against prior work.

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Physics-informed and graph learning.} Physics-guided learning adds scientific structure to data-driven models \cite{willard2022piml}.
Physics-informed networks penalize violations of governing equations \cite{raissi2019pinn}, and graph formulations have carried such constraints into flood forecasting \cite{taghizadeh2025floodpignn}.
GraphSAGE \cite{hamilton2017graphsage} learns from node neighborhoods inductively, which suits spatial grids split into separate regions.

\textbf{Mangrove carbon mapping.} Most mangrove carbon studies regress field or map values on spectral indices with random forests or boosted trees \cite{roy2024mangroveasiapacific}.
Global soil carbon products are themselves machine-learning outputs \cite{sanderman2018map}, whereas regional maps such as the southwest Florida map of Radabaugh et al.\ are calibrated on local field cores.
Geomorphic setting and tidal regime control mangrove soil carbon \cite{rovai2018controls,krauss2014sealevel}, and soil carbon maps can disagree substantially at fine scales \cite{holmquist2018tidal}.
Random cross-validation overstates accuracy on such data, so spatial blocking is required \cite{roberts2017cv,ploton2020spatial}.

\textbf{Uncertainty quantification.} Split conformal prediction gives finite-sample coverage under exchangeability \cite{lei2018conformal}, and conformalized quantile regression adapts the interval width to each input \cite{romano2019cqr} and has been applied to earth observation \cite{eo_conformal2024}.
Spatial dependence weakens exchangeability, which motivates localized (Mondrian) calibration \cite{vovk2005alrw,lou2024geoconformal}, whose validity needs dense calibration data in each region \cite{mao2024validmodelfree}.
Interval quality is scored with the Winkler interval score \cite{winkler1972interval,gneiting2007scoring}.

\textbf{Crediting and markets.} Blue carbon credits traded at a weighted average of US\$26.96 per credit in 2022 and 2023 \cite{farahmand2025bluecredits}, and registry records report issuances, retirements and buffer deposits per project \cite{vrod2026}.

\textbf{Research gap.} Table~\ref{tab:gap} compares these strands against five requirements of model-based crediting.
Carbon maps lack calibrated per-site intervals, conformal studies do not connect their intervals to a crediting rule, and registry rules set deductions and buffers without testing them against realized model error.
No study, to our knowledge, (i) converts calibrated spatial intervals into a registry uncertainty deduction, (ii) tests the registry's aggregation rule against realized errors, and (iii) checks the result against field cores and market records at the same time.

\subsection{Regions, data and graph}
Two southwest Florida mangrove regions were used: Ten Thousand Islands / Rookery Bay (R1; $-81.90^\circ$ to $-81.40^\circ$, $25.90^\circ$ to $26.30^\circ$) and the Everglades National Park coast (R2; $-81.20^\circ$ to $-80.90^\circ$, $25.30^\circ$ to $25.60^\circ$).
The label is the 30\,m soil organic carbon stock (0 to 100\,cm, t\,C/ha) of the 2020 global mangrove product of Maxwell et al., which also provides per-pixel 95\% prediction limits.
Native cells are aggregated into $5\times5$ blocks (about 150\,m) kept where the Global Mangrove Watch 2020 fraction exceeds 0.5 \cite{bunting2022gmw}, giving 10,124 nodes (64,366 directed 8-neighbor edges) in R1 and 22,326 nodes (143,034 edges) in R2.
Node features are ten Sentinel-2 L2A bands and derived indices \cite{drusch2012sentinel2} for 6 February 2026 and, with cloud masking, for 15 August 2026, together with elevation and distance to the tide gauge.
While revising this work, the Sentinel-2 clip used in the earlier version was found to be shifted 9.18\,km north of its true position, so that the mean normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) of mangrove nodes was 0.003.
Re-applying the same shift to correctly referenced imagery reproduced the earlier features (correlation 0.91 to 0.97), and all features were rebuilt (mean NDVI 0.46).

Splits are spatial: fine cells of about 2.5\,km are assigned whole to training, validation or test (R1: 6,465/1,583/2,076 nodes; R2: 14,499/3,351/4,476), and every model is trained and evaluated on subgraphs induced by one split, so no edge crosses a split boundary.

\subsection{Models and physics prior}
Baselines are linear regression, a location-only random forest, random forest with and without neighbor-mean (spatial lag) features, and XGBoost \cite{breiman2001rf,chen2016xgboost}.
The graph model is a two-layer GraphSAGE network (hidden size 16, dropout 0.3, Adam, learning rate 0.01, early stopping on held-out groups).
The physics-informed variant adds a steady-state prior,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{MSE}(\hat{y},y)+\lambda\,\mathrm{MSE}(\hat{y}_z,P_z)+w\,\overline{\mathrm{ReLU}(-\hat{y})^2},
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
where $P$ is an equal-weight, coefficient-free combination of tidal inundation frequency (the share of 2025 hourly National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) water levels above each node's U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) 3DEP lidar elevation, both in NAVD88) and red-edge canopy index (NDRE), reflecting the role of hydroperiod in sediment delivery and carbon preservation.
The prior in Eq.~\ref{eq:loss} replaced the NDVI and gauge-distance proxy of the earlier version.
The final candidate set added the unweighted mean of the physics-informed GNN and XGBoost (the ensemble), a stacked variant in which the GNN corrects out-of-fold XGBoost predictions, and a GNN trained on both image dates.
Table~\ref{tab:acc} lists every candidate with its accuracy.

\subsection{Uncertainty and crediting}
Quantile models (GNN, physics-informed GNN, XGBoost), normalized conformal random forest and a quantile ensemble were calibrated by split conformal prediction on the validation split, either globally or per spatial block (Mondrian, at least 30 calibration nodes per block).
A constant predictor with conformal calibration is reported as the trivial interval.
For node $i$ with estimate $\hat{y}_i$ and 90\% interval $[l_i,u_i]$, the relative uncertainty $U_i=(u_i-l_i)/(2\hat{y}_i)$ enters the VM0033 rule,
\begin{equation}
c_i=\hat{y}_i\,\bigl(1-\max(0,\,U_i-0.20)\bigr)\,(1-b),
\label{eq:credit}
\end{equation}
where $b$ is the registry's non-permanence buffer, applied unchanged.
Interval quality and deductions are summarized in Table~\ref{tab:uq} and mapped per node in Fig.~\ref{fig:map}.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
Feature sets, model settings, the physics prior and the interval method were chosen by spatial five-fold cross-validation (CV) on training and validation nodes, using selection rules written and committed before each experiment.
The test split was read once for all pre-specified models.
After that read, one further model (the ensemble) was pre-specified on cross-validation alone and evaluated in a second, declared test read, and its test numbers are marked in Table~\ref{tab:acc}.
Analyses that followed the first test read (generalization, two-date training, project aggregation, field cores, market data) used validation data or external data only and are reported as exploratory.
Nine logged deviations and all rules are archived with the code.

\section{Experiments and Results}

This section reports measurements only; their interpretation is deferred to Section~V.

\subsection{Accuracy}
Table~\ref{tab:acc} and Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(a) report accuracy.
In R1 the ensemble reached test $R^2=0.277\pm0.035$ (mean $\pm$ standard deviation (SD) over three seeds; mean absolute error (MAE) 58.1\,t\,C/ha), against 0.203 for XGBoost and 0.170 for the physics-informed GNN, whose seed SD (0.054) was a quarter of the plain GNN's (0.236).
A constant training mean scored $-0.11$, and the location-only model $-0.79$ despite a cross-validated $R^2$ of 0.42, so location alone did not transfer to unseen blocks.
In R2 all non-linear models scored between 0.56 and 0.60.
On interior test nodes at least two hops from any other split, the ensemble kept $R^2=0.19$ (R1) and 0.58 (R2).
With all other settings fixed, correcting the image georeferencing alone raised the GNN's cross-validated $R^2$ from 0.17 to 0.26.
The physics prior's correlation with the predictions it constrains was 0.14 on R1 test nodes, against 0.76 for the earlier proxy.

\subsection{Generalization and stability}
A single ensemble trained on both regions reached cross-validated $R^2=0.37$ (R1) and 0.53 (R2), against 0.47 and 0.55 for region-specific models, whereas models applied to a region they had not seen scored below zero (Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(b)).
In that zero-shot setting, conformal intervals calibrated on the source region covered 76\% to 89\% of target validation nodes, and recalibrating them on half of the target's validation nodes restored 88\% to 97\%.
Training the physics-informed GNN on both image dates raised the rank agreement of its predictions across dates from 0.44 to 0.88 and its $R^2$ with August imagery from $-0.29$ to 0.35, for a 0.02 loss with February imagery.

\subsection{Intervals and crediting}
Table~\ref{tab:uq} and Fig.~\ref{fig:results}(c) summarize the selected intervals (conformalized quantile GNN, global calibration).
In R1 the intervals matched the trivial interval's width but scored 18\% better on the Winkler score, and their width ranked absolute errors ($\rho=0.54$, 95\% confidence interval 0.51 to 0.58).
In R2 they were 32\% narrower than the trivial interval (191 vs.\ 282\,t\,C/ha).
Mondrian calibration covered 87.9\% in R1, where only six validation blocks were available.
Applied per node, the VM0033 rule withheld a median 12.7\% (R1) and 2.1\% (R2) of credits.
Across all five interval methods, credited stock stayed within 0.84 to 0.90 (R1) and 0.95 to 0.98 (R2) of the buffer-only amount.
Over the 2,076 R1 test nodes (about 4,700\,ha), the deducted share equals 0.71 million t\,CO$_2$e of standing stock, or US\$19.2 million at the 2022 and 2023 average credit price (Section~II).
This is an upper bound, because credits are issued on stock changes rather than on standing stock.
Interval width did not track mangrove loss between 2010 and 2020 (Spearman $-0.06$ in both regions).
Among the seven blue carbon projects with issued credits (6.62 million), the median buffer was 11.0\% and 73.5\% of credits had been retired (registry records, Section~II).

When node uncertainties were aggregated to 2.5\,km pseudo-projects as VM0033 prescribes (root-sum-of-squares, independent errors), the stated project uncertainty covered the realized aggregate error in 18\% (R1, 11 projects) and 33\% (R2, 45 projects) of cases and implied no deduction.
Treating errors as fully correlated covered 91\% and 100\% and implied median deductions of 14.6\% and 3.1\%.

\subsection{Field soil cores}
The 87 mangrove cores of the Coastal Carbon Library \cite{ccn2023library} were converted to stocks over the top 100\,cm, using a quadratic organic-matter conversion of the form in Craft et al.\ \cite{craft1991loi} fitted on 679 regional samples ($R^2=0.91$), and matched to nodes within 300\,m.
At the 25 sites, the median absolute error was 10.1\% for the ensemble, 11.6\% for the physics-informed GNN, 16.5\% for XGBoost, 14.1\% and 15.1\% for the Maxwell and Radabaugh maps, and 15.0\% for a constant.
The Maxwell map ranked sites best (Spearman 0.63; ensemble 0.29).
The two published maps were uncorrelated at node level ($-0.10$ in R1).
For the 13 cores sampled to at least 50\,cm, field-calibrated intervals covered 85\% to 92\% under leave-one-study-out and implied VM0033 deductions of 22\% to 29\%.
The label product's own 95\% prediction limits contained all 13 cores, but their median half-width equaled 101\% of the estimate.
Rescaled to 90\% under a normal approximation, they would imply a VM0033 deduction of about 65\% in both regions.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{The physics-informed GNN and tree ensembles are complementary.} Alone, the physics-informed GNN matched XGBoost in cross-validation (0.41 each), but their mean outperformed every other final candidate in cross-validation (0.47 and 0.55, against at most 0.42 and 0.53) and every model on test (Table~\ref{tab:acc}).
The GNN contributes neighborhood structure and the hydroperiod prior that the trees do not see, consistent with the geomorphic and tidal controls on mangrove soil carbon reviewed in Section~II, and it was also the most stable graph model across seeds and, once trained on both dates, across seasons.
The physics prior now acts as a weak constraint rather than a target the model copies.

\textbf{Estimation uncertainty is as large as the buffer the market already holds.} The per-node deduction in R1 (12.7\%) is similar to the realized median buffer of issuing blue carbon projects (11.0\%), yet today it is not applied.
Calibration is what makes the deduction usable, since the label map's uncalibrated limits would withhold about five times as much in R1 (Section~IV-D).
Fig.~\ref{fig:map} shows that the deduction concentrates in specific areas, so it is a location-dependent quantity that a flat buffer cannot represent.
Because width does not follow observed mangrove loss, the two discounts measure different risks and belong in separate factors, as in Eq.~\ref{eq:credit}.
\textbf{Business implication:} a registry can adopt the deduction without changing its buffer policy, and an issuer can see in advance which areas carry the largest deduction and where additional field sampling would raise the credited volume most.

\textbf{Aggregation under independent errors is the weak point of the registry rule.} Spatial model errors are correlated, so root-sum-of-squares aggregation shrinks project uncertainty and grants no deduction even where realized errors exceed the allowance.
A correlation-aware or empirically calibrated aggregation restored nominal coverage.
The same dependence explains why random validation overstates mapping accuracy and why Mondrian calibration undercovered with only six blocks, both anticipated by the spatial validation and conformal studies in Section~II.
Model-based crediting therefore needs an aggregation rule that accounts for spatial dependence.

\textbf{What the intervals can and cannot do.} Interval width is bounded by accuracy: with test $R^2=0.07$ for the GNN underlying the intervals in R1, at most about 4\% narrowing over the trivial interval is attainable, and in R2 ($R^2=0.58$) the 32\% achieved is close to the 35\% bound.
The value in R1 lies in adaptivity and Winkler score, not sharpness.
Local calibration is required when a model is moved to a new region.
Field-calibrated deductions exceed label-calibrated ones, a reminder that the label product carries its own error, in line with the fine-scale disagreement between soil carbon maps reported for United States tidal wetlands.

\section{Limitations}
\textbf{Labels are a modeled product:} the training label is a global map that disagrees with a field-calibrated regional map at node level.
\textbf{Accuracy in R1 is moderate} ($R^2=0.28$).
\textbf{The ensemble was evaluated in a second test read,} declared and limited to one pre-specified model.
\textbf{Single tide gauges} represent each region, and R2's is about 70\,km away.
\textbf{Field cores are sparse} (13 deep enough for 100\,cm stocks) and may overlap the global map's training data.
\textbf{Market outcomes} were not modeled, since prices and buffers come from published records.

\section{Conclusion}
Calibrated per-node uncertainty from a physics-informed GNN and XGBoost ensemble can supply the VM0033 uncertainty deduction.
Across two regions and two image dates, the ensemble reached test $R^2=0.28$ and 0.60, intervals reached 89\% to 92\% coverage, and the deduction (median 12.7\% in R1) is comparable to the 11.0\% buffers that issuing projects hold.
The methodology's independent-error aggregation covered only 18\% to 33\% of realized project errors, and against 87 field cores the ensemble's site-level error (10.1\%) was below both published maps and a regional mean.
Future work will test a correlation-aware aggregation with registry project data, add regions with denser field cores, and replace the steady-state prior with repeated elevation and accretion measurements.
